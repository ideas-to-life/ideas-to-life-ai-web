🤝 Strategic Exploratory Meeting

Reusable cheatsheet for high-impact conversations with CEOs, founders, boutique consultancies, partners, and senior technology leaders.

Relationship First Discover Before Positioning Enterprise Architecture AI Transformation Find the Intersection

🧭 1. Foundations & Mindset

1. The Objective
“Do not go in trying to sell. Go in trying to understand whether there is a valuable intersection.”

Primary goal: Understand the other person's business, clients, ambitions, capability model, and current challenges.

Secondary goal: Test whether your Enterprise Architecture + AI Transformation capability could extend what they already do.

Success criteria: A clear mutual opportunity or a concrete next conversation.

3. Start with Curiosity
1“I'd love to understand more about what you're building and where you're seeing the most interesting opportunities.”
2“What are you seeing in your clients at the moment?”
3“What made you interested in having this conversation?”
LISTEN FOR: Why the meeting is happening, what problem they are trying to solve, and whether they already have something specific in mind.

🔍 2. Deep Discovery

4. Understand Their Business
•“How would you describe your proposition today?”
•“Which types of clients and problems are you most focused on?”
•“What types of engagements are working particularly well?”
•“Where are you seeing demand change?”
AIM: Understand the business model, not merely the service catalogue.
5. Understand Their Ambition
•“Where do you want the business to go over the next couple of years?”
•“Are there capabilities you want to build or strengthen?”
•“What kinds of client problems would you like to be able to solve more deeply?”
•“What would make the biggest difference to the business?”
LOOK FOR: Growth, differentiation, new advisory services, specialist capability, trusted associate networks, strategic partnerships.
6. Understand the Capability Model
•“How do you normally bring specialist expertise into client engagements?”
•“Do you mainly build capabilities internally, or work with trusted specialists and partners?”
•“What makes someone a good fit for your client model?”
INSIGHT: Reveals whether the relationship could be advisory, associate-based, partner-led, subcontracted, or simply networking.
9. Probe for Client Demand
•“What problems are clients finding hardest to solve?”
•“Where do clients need more senior guidance?”
•“Where do strategy and execution tend to break down?”
•“Are clients asking about AI? If so, what are they actually trying to achieve?”
PRINCIPLE: Don't assume the answer is AI. Follow the client's actual problem.

🎯 3. Positioning & Framing

7. Introduce Yourself — When Earned

Once you understand their context, connect your experience directly to what you have heard:

“That resonates with some of the work I've been doing. My focus has increasingly been around helping organisations connect business strategy, architecture, governance, delivery, and AI into lasting organisational capability.”

Then pause and ask:

“Does that overlap with any of the challenges you're seeing?”
8. Your Core Positioning
“I help organisations navigate transformation by connecting business strategy, architecture, governance, delivery, and AI into lasting organisational capability.”

This is your anchor, not a rigid script. Adapt the language naturally to the conversation.

⚡ 4. AI & Transformation Journey

10. The AI Conversation
•“Is AI currently more of an experimentation topic, a strategy topic, or a transformation topic for your clients?”
•“Are clients struggling more with the technology or with how AI changes their operating model?”
•“How are they thinking about governance, architecture, and responsible adoption?”
Your differentiator: Treat AI as an enterprise transformation problem — not simply an LLM or technology implementation.

📊 5. Signals & Decision Rules

12. Strong Signals (Green Lights)
  • They describe a real client problem you can address.
  • They mention capability gaps, not just lack of capacity.
  • They want to strengthen strategic advisory services.
  • They need senior expertise without a permanent hire.
  • They see architecture and AI as connected to business transformation.
  • They suggest a specific next step.
13. Weak Signals / Caution (Red Flags)
  • Conversation quickly becomes day-rate resource supply.
  • They mainly need execution capacity.
  • There is no meaningful client demand for your capabilities.
  • Their business model does not support external specialists.
  • They want a generic “AI person” rather than transformation leadership.
  • There is no obvious next step or mutual value.
14. Avoid vs Prefer Matrix
Avoid Prefer
“I can provide Enterprise Architecture services.” “Where does architecture fit into the transformation problems your clients are facing?”
Leading with your CV. Establishing relevance through their problems.
Presenting a service catalogue. Exploring demand and capability gaps.
Assuming their website tells the whole story. Asking how the actual business operates.
Trying to close a deal. Finding a problem worth exploring together.

🤝 6. Collaboration Models & Next Steps

15. If They Ask: “What are you looking for?”
“I'm exploring where my experience can create the most value next. I'm particularly interested in working with organisations where Enterprise Architecture, transformation, and AI need to come together — either directly with clients or through strong advisory and consulting relationships.”

Then return the conversation: “I'm interested in understanding whether you see that kind of need in your world.”

16. If They Ask: “How could we work together?”

Don't commit to a model prematurely. Explore flexible avenues:

  • Specialist capability within an existing engagement
  • Trusted senior associate relationship
  • Joint advisory proposition
  • Co-developed client offering
  • Targeted discovery / assessment engagement
“I'd rather understand where the client need is first, then work out the model that makes sense.”
17. Three Questions to Remember
1. “What are your clients increasingly asking you for?”
2. “Where do you see the biggest capability gap or opportunity?”
3. “Where do you want the business to go next?”
18. Evaluating the Ideal Outcome

🌟 Best: A specific client problem, capability gap, or proposition worth exploring together.

👍 Good: Agreement to continue the conversation with a concrete purpose.

👌 Fine: A useful relationship with a clearer understanding of each other's worlds.

The meeting is successful when you leave knowing more than when you arrived — and the other person sees a credible reason to speak again.
19. Before You Leave
1“Based on what we've discussed, is there an area where you think it would be useful for us to explore this further?”
2“Would it be useful for me to put some thoughts around what that could look like?”
ACTION: If there is a concrete opportunity, capture the problem, client context, desired outcome, your role, their role, and the agreed next step.
